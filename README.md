# App Tooling Research

Survey + recommendation research for extracting purpose-built tooling out of an existing
TypeScript React app (React, Zustand, xstate, mqtt.js over WSS, REST APIs with reason-code
JSON bodies, vendored AsyncAPI/OpenAPI contracts, oxlint).

> **Frontier:** 0150-message-inbox chartered (D-0038) and its gating fact answered — sessions are `clean: false` with a persisted clientId, which falsifies 0060 A-5 and 0070 A-6 (both corrected in place) and makes 0150 live rather than a skip. Scope, volume and protocol now known — ~6 of ~40 topics at ~5 msg/s, MQTT 3.1.1, keepalive 30 s — so per-message ack deferral fits at a ~1–10% duty cycle, but [MQTT-4.6.0-2] forces all acks through one receipt-ordered release queue, and the replay burst turns out to be broker-capped and paced rather than the 18k flood first feared. The broker is **ActiveMQ Classic**, which maps JMS persistence to MQTT QoS with the ternary inverted (AMQ-7045, open since 2018) — but that branch is reached only when `ActiveMQ.MQTT.QoS` is absent, and **the 6 topics are fed by MQTT clients**, so the defect is off this path and the track is viable — *today*. **One or more producers may drop MQTT support**, which puts them back on it with no visible break: SUBSCRIBE still succeeds, the durable subscription still exists, messages still arrive, and only offline retention silently stops. So the premise is a runtime property, not a settled fact, and the design splits into a **client-owned guarantee nobody can revoke** (effect and identity commit atomically) and a **broker-dependent one that can lapse** (offline retention) — with a `packet.qos` assertion at ingress to make the lapse visible (new question 18). All ~6 candidates are QoS 1 today, so the durable path covers the set as drafted — but the other ~34 are a **mix of QoS 1 and 0**, and ActiveMQ makes a durable subscription out of *every* QoS-1 subscription on a `clean: false` session. Those topics are **already durable today**, accumulating offline and pinning journals with no inbox and no reaping — and at ~75% QoS 1 that is ~26 incidental durable subscriptions against the 6 intended, so **the unintended durable path is about eight times the intended one** and every 6-based figure in the plan is superseded. Demoting them is cheap in the client and strands the old `1:<topic>` subscription forever, since ActiveMQ keys durable subscriptions on `(clientId, "<QoS>:<topic>")` — audit and decommissioning are one procedure. **And the broker may not stay ActiveMQ**: a swap to **Mosquitto** is on the table, which does not lapse those findings so much as *invert* them — Mosquitto defaults to 1000 queued per client dropping **newest**, so the same config gives **~26 seconds** of offline retention against ActiveMQ's unbounded, while its 20-message in-flight window makes deferred acks far safer than ActiveMQ's ~3,200 outstanding. Generous retention with dangerous backpressure, versus safe backpressure with thin retention. New question 19 owns the compatibility matrix and a per-broker minimum-configuration checklist; the design rule is to express everything against MQTT 3.1.1 semantics and **detect at runtime what the protocol does not guarantee** — which raises the value of **D-0019's ordering stamp**, since per-topic sequence-gap detection is the one mechanism that catches Mosquitto's silent newest-drop, ActiveMQ's cap-discard, and a producer migration alike. Two ship-blockers belong to whoever owns the broker: abandoned durable subscriptions are never reaped (`offlineDurableSubscriberTimeout` defaults -1, the reaper is never constructed) and a full store blocks every publisher indefinitely. The clientId is device-scoped on a **static roster**, so in steady state nothing new is orphaned and the exposure concentrates at provisioning and decommissioning — which withdraws the `offlineDurableSubscriberTimeout` recommendation, since reaping a legitimately-absent client silently discards the backlog this work exists to preserve; a decommissioning procedure targets the real failure instead. Cross-reload durability genuinely works, so 0150 keeps both halves of its value. **The Mosquitto swap is 3–6 months out** — inside the service life of anything designed now — so question 19's premise is settled affirmative and the report designs for both (D-0039). How many of the ~26 incidental QoS-1 topics need offline retention is unknown; D-0039 rules **size for all, design for none**, and reframes the unanswerable question into one that is answerable by reading the handler: *is the effect accumulating or replacing?* **The spike ran ahead of the survey on user directive (D-0039) and is complete** — `spikes/durable-inbox`, 32 tests green. Its decisive check kills a real Chromium with SIGKILL mid-write and finds effects exactly equal to identities at every crash offset, while a deliberately two-transaction negative control reproduces the double-apply split — so **the central claim survives falsification and the falsifier demonstrably works**. It also answers three plan questions: the await-in-transaction footgun is made *unwritable* by a pure synchronous projection rather than policed by lint (better and cheaper than the rule the plan proposed), `durable` + `qos: 0` is unrepresentable via union arms, and the delivered-QoS guard costs one comparison and no fifth error class. One correction to the plan fell out: **hydration is protected by withholding the acknowledgement, not by withholding SUBSCRIBE**, since a resumed session's backlog arrives on CONNACK with no SUBSCRIBE issued. Seven things went unverified and are named rather than omitted — chiefly that the crash lane and the broker lane are never joined, and that neither production broker's backpressure was exercised. Calls waiting: which of the ~26 topics accumulate vs replace (intake 0150 item b — the input a demotion audit needs); **the producers' publish QoS** (same item, one empirical check: subscribe at QoS 1, publish through the real path, read `packet.qos`); **what enforces the single-connection invariant** (item f — link stealing is on, so two tabs on one device would fight); the ActiveMQ version (CVE floor 6.2.4+); and whether any of this reopens D-0015/D-0016. **0160-flight-recorder is chartered (D-0040)**: a memory-bounded, per-bucket capture of recent xstate transitions, MQTT messages, HTTP exchanges and custom event streams, bundled on an explicit app trigger or a global failure net into an owned OpenAPI-contracted payload and POSTed to the team's backend. The brainstorm fixed five rulings — passive triggers only, schema ours, a 10–50 MB envelope, retry-within-session as delivery's null (durable parking must earn its way against the RAM-disk risk), replay door-open only via ten reserved data-model decisions — and holds build and adopt+wrap at **equal merit**. Its grounding sweep found the slot empty in OSS (the real implementations live inside Sentry/Bugsnag), found **nothing in the field bounds memory by bytes**, and found **`fetch keepalive` postdates the Firefox ~124 floor** — so page-dismissal delivery is sendBeacon-or-nothing (≤ 64 KiB) and the design splits into two payload budgets. It also surfaced a device fact now in the app profile and larger than the track: **the OS runs from a RAM disk, so IndexedDB is RAM** — persistence never escapes the memory budget, power-cycle durability does not exist on-device, and 0150's guarantee also ends at a power cycle. The plan then survived a **D-0036-shape failure-mode enumeration** (2026-08-23, user-directed one-off): eight lanes, 152 modes, nine unowned — multi-tab capture topology and bundle identity the largest — plus two defects in the plan's own text (a torn-snapshot seal protocol, a retry-null contradiction), all amended in place with the register preserved verbatim at `tracks/0160-flight-recorder/plan-fmea-enumeration.md`, intake items i–l added, and the report now owing the same pass over its recommended design. **0160 passed its go gate (D-0041) and its report is drafted**, with the track-specific "Boundedness" criterion adopted at weight high. ## How it works

Track numbers run in two bands: **00xx–09xx** for tracks about the application's tooling,
and **99xx** for meta tracks about this repo's own research machinery (D-0025).

Each track lives in `tracks/NNNN-<slug>/` and moves through one lifecycle:
`research-plan.md` (key questions, candidates, rubric weights) → `report.md` (source-linked
survey + recommendation: **adopt / adopt + wrap / build / skip**, opening with an
ASD-STE100-style summary) → later `spikes/`. Track statuses: `planned → surveying →
report drafted → accepted`; `deferred` marks a track parked until scheduled. Acceptance is
recorded as a `D-####` entry in [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md). Questions only the user can
answer go through dated files in `intake/`. Run `node scripts/check-docs.ts` before
committing.

Accepted tracks may run **spikes**: standalone, exact-pinned npm packages under
`tracks/<track>/spikes/<slug>/`, scaffolded by `node scripts/new-spike.ts <track-dir> <slug>`
and isolated per the [spike harness spec](docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-14-spike-harness-design.md).
Each spike designs its interfaces first (`design.md`, per the vendored
[codebase-design skill](.claude/skills/codebase-design/SKILL.md)) and records go/no-go results
in `findings.md` — the durable artifact. A track may run a spike **earlier** than this on an
explicit user directive, pre-scoped by its research plan's key questions instead of by a report;
0150 did so under D-0039, which records why and what the spike does not settle.
